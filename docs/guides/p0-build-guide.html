Before you start

Bench, safety, and the order of building and bring-up

Bench

  • A clean, well-lit table, a soft mat or towel for the printed and anodised parts, and small trays for screws sorted by size.
  • Ventilation for soldering and the insert work; a window open is enough.
  • Your camera tripod (or a 3/8"-16 bolt through a wooden board) to stand the finished rig on.
  • A phone with a guitar-tuner app (belt tension) and a 200 g weight as a second check.

Safety, every time

  • Bench supply: set the current limit before you connect anything: 0.3 A for the first power-up (bring-up Stage B).
  • Touch a grounded metal object before handling a board; hold boards by their edges.
  • Soldering iron and hot air at 230–350 °C: park them in the stand, never on the table.
  • Loctite: gloves on, wipe excess at once.
  • When the drivers are on, the rig is clamped or standing on the tripod and your fingers stay away from the belt and pulleys.

Build and bring-up go hand in hand

The mechanics can start as soon as the parcels from JLCCNC, JLC3DP and StepperOnline arrive. The boards come later, and each is tested on the bench before it goes into the rig. The bring-up companion (next deliverable) carries those stages.

Checks

  • ST-303: the 45 mm pilot is clean and round; a caliper reads 45.00–45.03 mm (H7). No burrs on the 16 holes.
  • ST-208: outside diameter 9.98–10.00 mm (h7); the magnet seat at the end is clean.
  • Printed parts: no warping (lay the body on the table, it must not rock), holes open, no loose powder in the insert holes.
  • Motor: multimeter on ohms between black and green: about 1.4 Ω; between red and blue: about 1.4 Ω; black to red: open (no reading).
  • Reducers A and B: turn the input by hand; it should feel even all round, with no tight spot.

Do

  1. Fit the M3 or M4/M5 tip; heat to 230 °C.
  2. Set the insert on its hole, small end down. Rest the tip in it and let its weight sink it; guide it straight, do not push hard.
  3. Stop about 0.2 mm proud, lift the iron, and press it flush at once with the flat block, held square.
  4. Leave each insert a minute before the next one beside it. Do all the body's inserts now: they are needed for P1 anyway.
An insert that goes in crooked: heat it again and pull it out with the tip, let the part cool, and set a new one. Never force a screw into a crooked insert.

Checks

  • Every insert sits flush and square (lay a steel rule across: no rocking).
  • A screw turns in by hand to full depth in each one.

Do

  1. Turn the reducer so its 12 flange holes line up with the 12 inserts in the housing's rear flange.
  2. Press it into the bore from the output (ear) side, square and evenly, by hand or slowly in the vice, until its flange seats. Do not ream the ribs.
  3. Insert the 12 aluminium screws from the output face through the reducer into the inserts. Snug them all, then tighten in a criss-cross order to 0.6 N·m.
0.6 N·m is the maximum for these aluminium screws: set the Wera to 0.6 and stop at the click.

Checks

  • The reducer flange sits flat on the housing all round (no gap visible).
  • The input still turns by hand as evenly as before.

The 38 mm motors for P1 use other colours (black, blue, green, red). If the axis runs backwards later, swap nothing: direction is a firmware setting.

Do

  1. Cut the four motor leads to 80 mm from the motor body. Strip 2 mm.
  2. Crimp an SPH-002T-P0.5S contact on each with the PA-09 (die for 28–24 AWG). Push them into the PHR-4 housing in the order above until they click.
  3. Glue the thermistor bead to the motor case right next to the lead exit with a dab of thermal epoxy. Let it set as the tube says. Run its two leads with the motor leads.

Checks

  • Plug pins 1–2: about 1.4 Ω; pins 3–4: about 1.4 Ω; pin 1 to pin 3: open.
  • Thermistor leads: about 10 kΩ at room temperature (25 °C).
  • Pull each wire gently: no contact comes out of the housing.

Belt and tension

290 Hz on the long span, then lock the plate

Wera 0.3–1.2 N·m, 2 and 2.5 mm bitsTuner app200 g weight
JointScrewsKeyTorqueLocker
Belt tension screw1 × M3 × 12 ISO 47622.5 mm0.3 N·m maxnone
Motor plate to housing wall tops4 × M3 × 8 ISO 7380 + 4 washers2 mm0.6 N·mnone

Do

  1. Lay the belt over the 60T pulley. Set the motor plate on the wall tops with the belt round the 20T pulley; the four plate screws in, just finger-loose.
  2. Slide the 20T pulley up or down until both pulleys' flanges line up under a steel rule laid across them. Tighten its set screws on the flat (one drop of 243).
  3. Turn the tension screw until the long span, plucked in the middle, reads about 290 Hz on the tuner. Second check: 200 g hung at mid-span deflects it about 2 mm.
  4. Tighten the four plate screws to 0.6 N·m, then check 290 Hz again.

Checks

  • About 290 Hz after the plate screws are tight (the span carries 14 N).
  • Turn the 20T pulley by hand for a few turns: even, no tight spot, the belt runs in the middle of both pulleys and does not climb a flange.
  • Nothing rubs the belt (screw heads, housing walls).
Over-tension wears the motor bearing and raises the drag the slew tests measure. Stay near 290 Hz.

Do

  1. Display: read the pin labels printed on the module. It must be VCC, GND, SCL, SDA from pin 1 (the board's J11 has 3.3 V on pin 1, ground on pin 2). Solder it on the header's 2.5 mm spacer; cut the pin tails on the back to 0.6 mm.
  2. Battery lead: solder the mainboard half of ST-531-PWR (18 AWG, XT30U-F, 40 mm from the board) to the J1 pads: red to +, black to −. Heat-shrink both joints.
  3. Harness: build it from the 305 mm pre-crimped leads (sheet below): one lead per pin, 1:1, colours as the table. P0 keeps the full length; coil the spare and tie it.
  4. Board onto the bezel bosses, 4 screws, 0.3 N·m. Plug the harness into J7 (AZ) and the cartridge board's J1.
  5. Bezel onto the body, 6 screws, 0.5 N·m. Tie the harness to the floor anchor 20 mm from each plug.
A display with GND on pin 1 must not be fitted as it is: it would get 3.3 V backwards. Ask me first; the fix is moving two 0 Ω links (R96, R97 to R98, R99).

Checks

  • Harness continuity, all 15 pins, plug to plug (multimeter beep), and no pin beeps to its neighbour.
  • Board to bezel: the display glass does not touch the bezel window (about 0.7 mm in the CAD).

On the stand, leads off the axis

Tripod, a zero mark, and loose loops in the two leads

Do

  1. Screw ST-303's socket onto the tripod's 3/8" screw, firm by hand. Level the tripod.
  2. Stick a strip of tape across the joint between the body and ST-303 and draw one line over both: that is your zero mark.
  3. Bench lead: XT30U-M with red and black 18 AWG to the supply's banana sockets. Plug it into the board's XT30. A USB-A to USB-C cable from the PC to port A.
  4. Leave each lead a loose loop of about 30 cm so the body can turn half a turn either way. Unwind after any full-turn test.

Checks

  • The body turns ±180° from the zero mark without pulling either lead.
  • Nothing hangs from the plugs; the leads are tied to the tripod leg.
For the freezer test later, the rig goes in on a small board with the 3/8" bolt, the two leads through the door seal.

Final checks before power

Then bring-up Stage D

  • Every joint of sheets 3 to 11 torqued (mark each screw head with a paint pen as you go).
  • No loose screw, washer or wire offcut inside the body: shake it gently and listen.
  • Belt still about 290 Hz.
  • Bench supply set to 7.4 V with the limit at 0.3 A, output off, before you plug in.
  • Hands and cables clear of the belt; the rig stands steady on the tripod.

Stage D (from the bring-up companion): encoder reads, thermistor reads, drivers at 0.3 A on the 10 V rail, slow steps, then the tracking and slew tests whose logs feed the P0 measurements.

R

Swapping in reducer B

After the P0 measurements with A (D82)

Take out

  1. Power off; unplug the bench lead and USB. Unplug the harness at the cartridge board's J1.
  2. Remove ST-303 (16 screws) with the tube still on it. Remove the 4 M4 screws and lift the cartridge out.
  3. Remove the carrier (2 screws); unplug the motor. Loosen the plate screws, take the belt off, lift the motor plate away.
  4. Remove the 60T pulley (4 screws) and the 12 aluminium screws. Push reducer A out from the rear evenly with a soft drift (wood or plastic), working round.

Put back with B

  1. Sheets 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 10 in order, with B. Fresh Loctite 243 on every screw that had it; clean the old off the threads first.
  2. Run the encoder calibration again before measuring: the map belongs to the reducer and tube as assembled.
  3. Keep A's measurements; the better reducer goes on azimuth in P1 and the other on altitude.
